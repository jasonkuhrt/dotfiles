# Logo design

A mark reaches Jason only after it passes the gates below, in order. A mark
that fails one is fixed or dropped. It is never shown with a note saying it
fails. Jason, 2026-11-10: "Can you please not show me logo marks that
obviously fail once they become small? I mean, that should just be a given".

Speed cuts polish, never a gate. "Broad", "fast" and "rough" ask for more
ideas drawn less carefully; each idea is still screened before it is shown.

## The gates

1. **Small.** Render the mark fitted into a box 110 pixels wide by 32 tall,
   on white. Every letter of the name can still be told apart, and nothing
   in it has turned to speckle.
   - A descriptor line that sits apart from the mark (a tagline, "Fondation"
     under the letters) may drop out of the small form.
   - A word set inside the mark's own shapes (on a ring, along a stem, in a
     bar) fails, unless the mark still holds with the word taken out. Then
     the small form is the mark without it.
   - Thin outlines, a mark that fills a small part of its container, and a
     tall narrow stack all shrink their letters: check them first.
2. **One colour.** Flattened to black on white, the name still reads. Where
   letters overlap, the one-colour form leaves a gap between them.

The box is the agent's threshold (2026-11-10): about a retina favicon, or a
small avatar or header mark in a list. Change it when Jason names a real
smallest use.

## Showing a mark

- Each mark shown carries its small form beside it, at its real pixel size,
  so the gate is visible and not claimed.
- When an existing mark is the one to beat, it sits on the same sheet.

## Briefing another agent

The gates go into the brief in full. A brief never waives one to save time.

- Bad (2026-11-10): a "broad, fast" round of 24 thumbnails from two streams,
  briefed with "no one-colour versions, no small-size checks". Fourteen
  failed at 32 pixels: a seal whose letters shrank to a few pixels, letters
  drawn as thin outlines, words set on a ring and along a stem.
- Good: the same 24 screened at 32 pixels first, and the nine that read
  shown, each beside its 32-pixel form.
